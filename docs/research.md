# Research and provenance

Research date: **2026-10-15**. This note records observed sources and separates them from editorial decisions. It is not a promise that every external page remains unchanged.

## Exam baseline

The [English study guide](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/resources/study-guides/az-900) was read in full. It labels its skills effective **July 20, 2026**, and its page metadata reports **June 22, 2026**. The [certification page](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/azure-fundamentals/) independently reports the July update. There is no later effective scope on the retrieved guide.

The 57 illustrative skills bullets map to 11 subdomains and three weighted domains: cloud concepts (15 bullets, 25–30%), architecture and services (27, 35–40%), management and governance (15, 30–35%). Each bullet receives one editorial competency; compound bullets retain their named services. This is the approved scope baseline, not a prediction of every tested detail. The guide says related topics can occur and notes that commonly used preview features may appear.

Its change table flags minor updates in compute/networking, management/deployment and monitoring. It does not identify exact changed bullets. We have not reconstructed a previous official version, so no fabricated per-competency history is presented. The baseline can detect future reviewed differences.

The official certification page currently gives **45 minutes**, includes **Portuguese (Brazil)** among available languages, and links to the [free Practice Assessment](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/azure-fundamentals/practice/assessment?assessment-type=practice&assessmentId=23&practice-assessment-type=certification) and an exam-environment sandbox. The guide warns that localization normally follows English updates by roughly eight weeks and can lag further. The [scoring policy](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/exam-scoring-reports) explains that 700 is a **scaled** passing score; it does not mean 70% correct. Recheck logistics and local price at booking. No exact question count, guaranteed topic distribution or passing probability is inferred.

## Learning ecosystem

The [three official learning paths](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/training/azure) provide the primary teaching progression. Course titles now use “Introduction to Cloud Infrastructure”; the credential remains Azure Fundamentals. Course modules can teach material beyond the guide (including AI/ML/IoT references), so training content cannot automatically redefine exam scope.

The [MicrosoftLearning lab repository](https://github.com/MicrosoftLearning/AZ-900-Microsoft-Azure-Fundamentals) provides hands-on context. Public instructions do not imply an unrestricted free Azure subscription. Portal procedures may drift; users must inspect charges and cleanup steps.

[John Savill's repository](https://github.com/johnthebrit/AZ900CertCourse) links his course playlist and handout. It is selected for explanatory format, not certification authority; individual videos were not exhaustively audited. [Ricardo Martins' Portuguese guide](https://github.com/ricmmartins/guia-estudo-az900) reports a February 20, 2026 revision, preceding the July guide. It usefully points to Portuguese documentation but omits or aggregates some current bullets and includes older scope examples such as the TCO calculator. Use current official sources when these differ. No third-party content was copied into the repository.

The catalog deliberately avoids paid simulators, unaudited mass question banks and exam dumps. “Best” navigation recommendations mean editorial fit for a purpose; no comparative accuracy study was performed. Publication dates left null are unknown, not estimated. Verification records explicitly distinguish reviewed landing pages from uninspected interactive assessments.

## Modeling decisions and limits

JSON permits dependency-free ingestion and repeatable validation. One competency per source bullet keeps coverage auditable without reproducing long source passages. The graph, misconceptions and assessment criteria are authored pedagogical judgments. They are not Microsoft's prerequisite graph or a validated psychometric model. Official teaching-path references support each area; precise claims about changing prices, availability or licensing still require direct product-documentation checks when teaching.

Coverage is structural. The initial bank samples the curriculum rather than supplying a unique validated item for all 57 competencies. Live tutor behavior requires separate qualitative evaluation; deterministic data checks cannot prove factual or instructional quality. Resource link success cannot prove continued semantic validity.

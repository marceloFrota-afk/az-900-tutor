# Original practice bank

`questions.json` contains **65 original Portuguese scenarios covering all 57 primary competencies** in curriculum version `2026-07-20`: 17 items in cloud concepts, 30 in Azure architecture/services, and 18 in management/governance. Seven are multiple choice with feedback for every distractor; the remainder ask for explanation, comparison or application. Every item requests reasoning. These are teaching exercises, not recalled confidential Microsoft exam questions.

Q001–Q030 retain their IDs and primary mappings. Q031–Q057 close the former 27 coverage gaps. Q058–Q065 provide changed-scenario transfer checks on availability/scalability/elasticity, authentication/authorization, RBAC/Policy/locks, budgets, observability, redundancy/recovery, shared responsibility, and zones/regions. `transfer_variant` marks these extra prompts; `skill_type: transfer` identifies the intended evidence. A variant does not establish delayed retrieval until actually administered on a later date without assistance or prior exposure.

Each item maps one primary competency to official Microsoft conceptual sources. Cross-topic scenarios do **not** automatically produce evidence for every concept mentioned. Tutors can translate prompts while retaining service names, constraints, distinctions and scoring criteria. Sources support the concepts, not Microsoft authorship or endorsement of the questions. `verified_at` means author source inspection, not independent validation.

Q001–Q030 had a second-agent editorial review on 2026-10-15. It clarified the SMB requirement in Q016 (Blob supports NFS in some configurations) and Reader control-plane scope in Q021. The expansion's author inspected official conceptual sources and self-reviewed all 65 items. A separate AI reviewer subsequently read all 65 and corrected Q013, Q032, Q050 and Q058; Q031–Q065 metadata records that review. See the [dated review record](../docs/questions-review-2026-10-15.md) for exact scope and evidence. Human Azure subject-matter review, learner trials, difficulty calibration and item-discrimination statistics remain pending.

Present only prompt/options before the response; keep keys, rubrics and rationale tutor-side. Give feedback afterward, unless a simulation defers it. Normalize the 0–3 rubric by dividing by 3 before recording a CLI score. A correct letter without explanation is recognition evidence and cannot receive full rubric credit. A contradiction in the learner's reason triggers misconception repair even if the chosen option was right. Use the item's criterion as the required evidence; partial-credit prose does not override that criterion.

Coverage means that a scenario is available for every curriculum competency, **not** that one scenario exhausts a multi-part objective, that a learner has demonstrated it, or that the bank is a balanced official mock exam. Select by uncovered/weak competencies and domain weights; the bank's item counts do not reproduce exam weights. Repeated exposure makes scores less informative. Readiness needs fresh scenarios, delayed retrieval and multiple distinct attempts; a practice percentage does not translate to the official scaled score or a probability of passing.

Run `python scripts/validate.py` and `python -m unittest discover -s tests -v`. The coverage regression tests check mappings, stable original IDs, distinct transfer prompts, and rejection of a missing competency even when all domains remain represented. Structural checks cannot determine whether a rubric is pedagogically effective.

Generate exact current distributions and per-competency coverage with `python scripts/coverage.py`.

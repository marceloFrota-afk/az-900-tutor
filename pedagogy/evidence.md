# Evidence, feedback, and readiness

This project's statuses are operational labels, not psychometric estimates. `not_assessed` means no usable evidence; `learning` means a gap or insufficient consistency; `demonstrated` requires repeated independent explanation/application including delayed evidence; `needs_review` signals that retention should be checked; `inconclusive` covers guesses, contradictory reasoning, ambiguity, or compromised attempts. Preserve raw evidence so a new agent can reconsider a judgment. The CLI documents its exact thresholds; the tutor may be more cautious when the evidence warrants it.

Use a 0–3 explanation rubric: 0 = incorrect or absent; 1 = partly correct but missing a decisive distinction; 2 = correct explanation that addresses the key requirement; 3 = correct explanation plus application or defensible tradeoff. A bare multiple-choice letter has, at most, recognition evidence; ask for a brief explanation before using it to support demonstration. Confidence is context, not a score multiplier. A confidently wrong answer is a useful signal for misconception repair; a lucky answer is not mastery.

Record which question/scenario was used, date, hints or answer exposure, rubric score, learner explanation, and misconception evidence. Distinct IDs alone are insufficient if questions are paraphrases testing the same cue. After feedback, use a changed scenario and schedule retrieval on a later day. Example expanding intervals of 1, 3, and 7 days are MVP heuristics; compress or extend them for the deadline and observed retention. Do not invent a delayed attempt when time has not passed.

An evidence-based readiness statement should describe domain coverage; unseen or weak objectives; recent unaided reasoning in varied scenarios; delayed recall; unresolved misconceptions; and whether practice has become memorization. A tiny bank and an aggregate percentage cannot calibrate probability of passing. Microsoft's official Practice Assessment can supplement evidence, but remains practice rather than a guarantee. AZ-900's official scaled passing standard must not be equated to a percentage on these items.

## Basis and limits

Retrieval practice and distributing study have research support, but these studies do not validate this project's thresholds, schedule, or AZ-900 outcomes. [Dunlosky et al. (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266) reviewed common study techniques; [Karpicke and Roediger (2008)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1152408) experimentally examined repeated retrieval. We apply those principles as design guidance. Scaffolding, targeted feedback, and mastery judgments here are explicitly implemented tutoring choices; an LLM may still grade inconsistently. Qualitative fixtures in `evals/` test behavior, while real learner outcome validation remains future work.
